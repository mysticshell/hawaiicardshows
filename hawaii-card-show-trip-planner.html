Planning a Hawaii Trip? The Collector's Guide to Card Shows & Shops

Coming to the islands and want to work some cards into the trip? Here's how to time your visit around a show, which card shops are worth a detour, and how to get your pickups home in one piece — for Pokemon, sports cards, Magic, and every TCG in between.

Most people don't fly to Hawaii for the cards. But if you collect, there's a good chance your trip overlaps with a show — Oahu alone runs more than ten card events a month, and there's something happening somewhere across the islands almost every week. A card show is also one of the best ways to meet locals and find cards you won't see in any mainland shop, from island-exclusive promos to deals from collectors who don't sell online.

This is the visiting collector's version of our complete Hawaii card shows guide. If you've never been to a card show at all, start with our beginner's guide first — it covers what to expect and how to buy or sell without feeling lost.

Time Your Trip Around a Show

The single best move: check the calendar before you book. Shows in Hawaii are often announced on short notice and many are one-time or irregular, so the live calendar is the only reliable source. If you have any flexibility on dates, lining your trip up with a flagship weekend is worth it.

Browse the full Hawaii card show calendar → It goes as far out as shows are booked, filterable by island, so you can see exactly what's on while you're here.

The flagships worth planning around: the Aloha Card Show (annual, Blaisdell Center — among the biggest in the state), HNL Card Fest (two days at the Hawaii Convention Center), West Side Card Show (West Oahu, drew 9,000+ in 2026), and the recurring Keep It Aloha Card Show at SALT Kakaako. On Maui, The Collectors Hale is a three-day August event. Dates move year to year — confirm on the calendar.

Oahu — Where Most Visitors Land

If your trip is Waikiki-based like most first visits, you're in luck: Oahu has the highest density of shops and shows in Hawaii, and the best ones are a short rideshare from the tourist core. If you only have time for one show or one shop, make it Oahu. For the full breakdown, see our complete guide to card shows on Oahu.

Shops near the tourist core

These are the most visitor-friendly stops — close to Waikiki, Ala Moana, and downtown.

Space 62 (Ala Moana Center) ToyLynx (Honolulu) TCG Tavern (Isenberg) Aloha Card Shop Other Realms From the Heart

Space 62 sits right inside Ala Moana Center — the easiest card stop for any visitor, no car needed. ToyLynx (by Costco on Iwilei) has one of the deepest single-store TCG inventories in the state and a steady tournament schedule if you want to play while you're here. See every Oahu shop in the shops directory.

The shows to catch on Oahu

Recurring Oahu shows that visitors can usually count on hitting:

  • Keep It Aloha Card Show — recurring at SALT at Our Kakaako, a walkable shopping-and-food district. 70+ vendors, evening hours, strong Pokemon presence.
  • Paradise Card Show — recurring at The Republik in Honolulu. Big vendor floor, keiki goodie bags, sponsor giveaways.
  • Aloha Card Show & HNL Card Fest — the marquee convention-scale weekends. If one lands during your trip, build a morning around it.

Neighbor Islands — Maui, Big Island & Kauai

Island-hopping? Each neighbor island has its own small-but-real scene. Don't expect Oahu's density, but a well-timed neighbor-island show is a great way to meet local collectors.

Maui

The Valley Isle's scene is driven by a committed core of organizers. Maui Sports Cards in Kahului hosts regular trade nights and shows, and The Collectors Hale is a three-day August event worth planning around. Shops: Maui Sports Cards YoCards

Big Island — Hilo & Kona

The Big Island splits east (Hilo) and west (Kona), and it's a couple hours between them — so plan by the side you're staying on. Big Island Breaks in Kailua-Kona is the main west-side shop (Pokemon-heavy), while Hilo has TCG Hawaii and others running regular trade nights. Shops: TCG Hawaii (Hilo) Big Island Breaks (Kona) Slow Your Roll 4 Pillars

Kauai

The smallest scene of the four, but a tight community. Activity centers on a couple of shops, with occasional pop-up swaps. Shops: Crow's Nest

Travel Tips for Collectors

Bring cash

Most vendors take Venmo or Zelle, but cash still moves deals fastest and gives you the best leverage on a negotiation. ATMs at shows are rare — pull cash before you go.

Getting cards home safely

If you're buying singles or slabs, pack a few toploaders and a small team bag in your suitcase before you fly — you'll want them. For anything valuable, carry it in your carry-on, never checked luggage. Buying a graded card or a big sealed box? Many local shops will ship it to your home address so you're not nursing it through TSA — just ask at the counter.

Island-exclusive finds

Hawaii vendors often have inventory you'll never see at a mainland show — Japanese product brought in through local channels, island promos, and collections from sellers who don't list online. Leave room in the budget for something you didn't plan on.

Rent a car for the good shops

A few of the best shops and most shows sit outside the Waikiki core. If cards are a real priority for the trip, a rental car (or planning rideshare budget) opens up West Oahu shows like the West Side Card Show at the Kroc Center that you'd otherwise miss.

How the Hawaii Scene Works

The thing visitors notice first: it's friendly. The Hawaii card community is unusually tight — organizers support each other's shows, shop owners collaborate, and the aloha spirit carries onto the show floor. A few unspoken norms make you a welcome guest rather than a tourist:

  • Talk story. Deals here happen through conversation, not just price tags. Ask how someone's collection came together; you'll get better prices and better cards.
  • Respect the locals-first vibe. This is a community scene, not a flipping circuit. Lowballing every table to resell on the mainland reads loud and clear.
  • Support the keiki tables. Many shows run kid-focused giveaways and dollar bins — they're the heart of the scene.
