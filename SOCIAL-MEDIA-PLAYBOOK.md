# Social Media + YouTube Playbook
## Card Show Beginner's Guide + Etiquette Pillar

The two pillar pages we just shipped — [/what-is-a-card-show.html](what-is-a-card-show.html) and [/card-show-etiquette.html](card-show-etiquette.html) — are designed to be sliced into months of social content. This playbook breaks down exactly what to film, post, and chop, in priority order.

---

## Strategy in one paragraph

The pillar articles are the **source of truth**. Everything else (YouTube long-form, Shorts/Reels, IG carousels, TikTok, Twitter threads) is just **chops of that source content**, formatted for each platform. The goal isn't unique content per channel — it's **distribution leverage**. One filming session + one carousel design session should produce 6–8 weeks of ready-to-post material, all driving back to the same two SEO-optimized pages.

**Why this works:** social engagement → site traffic → newsletter signups → return-traffic via newsletter UTMs. Every piece of content has a path back to the site.

---

## Tier 1: YouTube long-form video (the source film)

This is the single biggest leverage point. One ~12-minute video can be chopped into 30+ pieces of derivative content.

### Video concept
**Title:** "Card Show Etiquette: 10 Rules Every Collector Should Know"
**Length:** 10–12 minutes
**Format:** Tyler on camera + B-roll from past Hawaii shows + occasional text overlays

### Why this video instead of "What Is a Card Show?"
The etiquette piece is **more shareable**. "What is a card show" answers a question, but "the unwritten rules" is **a take** — it's the kind of content people send to friends ("you should watch this before our trip to Paradise"). It's also better for SEO on YouTube because "card show etiquette" + "card show negotiation" are searched but underserved.

### Video structure

| Segment | Length | What's on screen |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (0:00–0:15)** | 15s | "If you've ever walked up to a card show vendor and felt awkward, this is for you. Ten rules nobody tells you about." | Tight on Tyler, energetic |
| **Intro (0:15–0:45)** | 30s | Quick frame: "We've been to every major show in Hawaii. These rules will save you money and get you better deals." Show clips from Paradise, Keep It Aloha, Bayview |
| **Rule 1: Ask before you touch (0:45–1:30)** | 45s | Tyler on camera + B-roll of a vendor pulling a card from a glass case for a buyer |
| **Rule 2: Don't lowball (1:30–2:30)** | 60s | The 10–25% explanation. Show overlay graphic: "$100 card → fair opening offer = $75–$90" |
| **Rule 3: "What's your best?" (2:30–3:15)** | 45s | Tyler demonstrates the phrase. Show overlay: "WHAT'S YOUR BEST?" big letters |
| **Rule 4: Bundle for better prices (3:15–4:00)** | 45s | B-roll of someone laying out 4 cards at a vendor table |
| **Rule 5: Don't camp at busy tables (4:00–4:45)** | 45s | Show a busy show floor (use KIA show floor photo as static, or pan over a clip) |
| **Rule 6: Don't talk down a card (4:45–5:45)** | 60s | Tyler explains. Maybe show the difference between two openings |
| **Rule 7: Cash gets cash prices (5:45–6:45)** | 60s | Tyler with cash in hand. Explain the 3% fee math. |
| **Rule 8: Don't snipe a negotiation (6:45–7:30)** | 45s | Tyler explains. Could overlay a "DON'T DO THIS" graphic |
| **Rule 9: Be a regular face (7:30–8:30)** | 60s | Story-format. Tyler talks about a vendor relationship at Paradise/KIA |
| **Rule 10: Tip the breakers (8:30–9:15)** | 45s | Show a breaker stream / clip of a giveaway |
| **Closer + CTA (9:15–10:00)** | 45s | "These 10 rules → real relationships → better deals. Drop a comment with your show story. Subscribe + check hawaiicardshows.com for the full guide." |

### Production notes for Tyler
- **Film vertical-friendly framing.** Even when shooting horizontal for YouTube, keep the subject (Tyler) center-frame so vertical 9:16 crops work for Shorts/Reels.
- **B-roll list to grab at upcoming shows:**
  - Wide shot of show floor (we have these)
  - Close-up of vendor pulling a card from a case for a buyer
  - Close-up of two collectors trading at a trade table
  - Cash being handed across a table
  - Hand flipping through a binder
  - A group around a breaker stream
- **Audio:** lapel mic minimum. Bad audio kills retention.
- **No script reading.** Bullet outline + improvise. Authentic > polished, per our brand voice.
- **Thumbnail:** Tyler's face with surprised/serious expression + bold text overlay "10 Card Show Rules" in our brand orange. CTR depends 70% on thumbnail.

### YouTube SEO setup
- **Title:** `Card Show Etiquette: 10 Unwritten Rules Every Collector Needs to Know`
- **Description (first 150 chars):** Open with the article URL + a punchy summary. YouTube indexes the first 150 chars heavily.
- **Tags:** card show, card shows, card show etiquette, card show tips, how to negotiate at a card show, Pokemon card show, sports card show, Hawaii card shows
- **End screen:** link to /what-is-a-card-show.html (full beginner's guide) + subscribe button
- **Cards (mid-video):** at the 5:00 mark, link to the article
- **Pinned comment:** "Read the full guide → hawaiicardshows.com/card-show-etiquette"

## Tier 2: YouTube Shorts / IG Reels / TikTok (chops)

From the single 10-minute video above, you get **at minimum 12 individual Shorts/Reels** — one per rule + 2 sizzle/hook variants. Each runs 30–45 seconds.

### Posting cadence
- **YouTube Shorts:** 2x/week
- **Instagram Reels:** 2x/week (post 24–48 hours after Shorts to avoid algorithm de-duplication penalty)
- **TikTok:** 2x/week (different hook angles)

That's **~6 posts per week from one filming session.** Sustained for 4 weeks = 24 pieces of content from one afternoon of filming.

### The 10 individual rule clips (drops on Shorts/Reels)

Each clip is structured the same way:
1. **Hook (0–3s):** "Don't do this at a card show:" or "The phrase that gets you better deals at card shows:"
2. **Rule explanation (3–25s):** The rule + the *why*
3. **CTA (25–30s):** "Full guide in bio → hawaiicardshows.com/card-show-etiquette"

### Sample hooks (one per rule, ranked by virality potential)

| Rule | Hook line (first 3 seconds — most important) |
|---|---|
| 1. Ask before touching | "If you grab a card without asking at a card show, the vendor noticed." |
| 2. Don't lowball | "If a card is $100 and you offer $40, you're not negotiating. You're insulting them." |
| 3. "What's your best?" | "This one phrase will get you better deals at every card show you go to." ⭐ HIGHEST POTENTIAL |
| 4. Bundle | "Want a discount at a card show? Don't ask for one. Do this instead:" |
| 5. Don't camp | "The most annoying buyer at a card show isn't the lowballer. It's this person:" |
| 6. Don't talk down | "Stop doing this if you actually want vendors to give you good prices:" |
| 7. Cash prices | "Why your card show price went up at checkout (and how to avoid it):" |
| 8. Don't snipe | "Walking into someone else's negotiation at a card show is a death sentence." |
| 9. Be a regular | "The collectors getting the best deals at card shows aren't who you think." |
| 10. Tip the breakers | "Tipping at a card show? Yes — and here's why it matters." |

### "Bonus" clips beyond the 10 rules (extra pulls from the videos)

- **The "first 60 seconds" intro** as a standalone Reel — a pure hook that drives traffic to the full video. Caption: "Watch the full thing on YouTube"
- **A "chapter recap" supercut** that flashes all 10 rules in 60 seconds with text overlays. Great for repost cycles months later.
- **The opener** ("we've been to every major show in Hawaii") repackaged as a credibility piece for sponsor pitches and the press kit.

## Tier 3: Instagram carousels (deeper, no filming required)

Carousels rank on Instagram differently than Reels — they convert more saves and shares. Build 4 over a single design session and post them between Reels for variety.

### Carousel ideas (in priority order)

**Carousel 1: "The 10 Card Show Rules" (10 slides, one per rule)**
- Slide 1: Title card — "10 Unwritten Rules of Card Shows"
- Slides 2–11: One rule per slide with the title and a 1-sentence summary
- Slide 12: CTA — "Save this for your next show. Full guide → link in bio."
- **Designed once, reusable forever.** Repost every 3 months with a fresh caption.

**Carousel 2: "What to Bring to Your First Card Show" (8 slides)**
- Pulled directly from the pillar's "What to Bring" section
- Each slide = one item with an icon (cash, want list, comps app, binder, comfy shoes, water, patience)
- Final slide CTA → /what-is-a-card-show.html

**Carousel 3: "5 Mistakes Beginners Make at Card Shows" (6 slides)**
- Title + 5 mistakes from the pillar's "Common Mistakes" section
- Mistakes are inherently shareable (negative-framed > positive-framed for engagement)

**Carousel 4: "Card Shows in Hawaii by Island" (6 slides)**
- Slide 1: "Card Shows in Hawaii — All 4 Islands"
- Slides 2–5: One per island (Oahu, Maui, Big Island, Kauai) with the show count + featured shows
- Slide 6: CTA → calendar
- **This one is the biggest local SEO + community-building play.** Saves and shares from neighbor-island accounts especially.

### Design specs (so you can hand off to designer or self-serve in Canva)
- 1080×1080 (square) or 1080×1350 (portrait — better Reels-feed real estate)
- Brand fonts: Phudu (headlines), Archivo (body)
- Brand colors: --ocean #1a6b5a, --lava #d4582a, --sand #f5f0e8
- Rule numbers in big Phudu, rule titles in white on ocean-green background
- Bottom-right: small `@hawaiicardshows` watermark + link in bio

## Tier 5: Twitter / X / Threads (low effort, link-driving)

- One thread per rule = 10 threads, posted across 5 weeks (2/week)
- Each thread: hook tweet → 4–6 tweets explaining → final tweet with link
- Cross-post to Threads identically
- Low audience right now but the threads are evergreen and easy to repost

## Measuring what works

Every social link should be UTM-tagged. Suggested taxonomy:
- `utm_source=youtube` (or instagram, tiktok, etc.)
- `utm_medium=social`
- `utm_campaign=card-show-etiquette` (or `card-show-guide`)
- `utm_content=rule-3-whats-your-best` (the specific clip/post)

After 4 weeks, pull GA4 by `utm_campaign` to see which clips drove the most traffic. Double down on the winners.
