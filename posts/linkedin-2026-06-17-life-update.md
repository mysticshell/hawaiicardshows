## The Post

In 2020 I was about to start a master's in international education.

Today I run a calendar for card shows in Hawaii.

Here's how that happened.

The plan was study abroad programs. Send students across borders, build cross-cultural relationships, nurture the next generation of leaders who actually knew each other. I figured this was the career.

Then COVID hit and international ed fell off a cliff.

So I looked sideways. Spent the next five years building digital community products with founders across SE Asia, Australia, and the Philippines. Web3, gaming, AI — the whole tour.

The work was great. But the longer I spent building communities online, the more I noticed people craving the opposite: actual in-person gathering.

Then, almost by accident, I found my way back to it.

A friend invited me to play golf in Hawaii. Same morning, same golf course, a card show was happening. I went and immediately got hooked. Spent the past year vending shows, helping organize events at golf courses and music venues, and getting to know every card shop on the island.

Whole time I kept running into the same problem: finding card shows here was nearly impossible. Instagram-only. Group-chat-only. Sometimes nothing at all.

So I built **Hawaii Card Shows** — the free community calendar for trading card events across the islands. Started as a personal rolodex. Turned into a two-week AI-assisted build. And now:

→ 7,500+ unique visitors
→ 27,000+ pageviews
→ 77 events tracked across all four Hawaiian islands
→ 72% of traffic from organic Google search
→ 120+ newsletter subscribers

Zero paid marketing. Two and a half months in. All organic, all word of mouth, all SEO.

The full story of how international ed → web3 → Hawaii card shows fits together is wild, and I'll write that one up properly soon. For now — if you're in the card show space, building community, or curious how community moves from digital to physical, let's connect.

🔗 hawaiicardshows.com

## Why this hook over the alternatives

**The contradiction opener** (master's in international ed → card show calendar) creates a curiosity gap LinkedIn can't ignore. Two specific career states on opposite ends of the spectrum, separated by one line. The brain needs to know how A became B.

**"Here's how that happened"** is a receipt — it promises a delivery, not just a tease.

Three lines, all above LinkedIn's mobile "see more" fold. Optimal for thumb-stopping.
